What Is RTP, and Why It Matters More Than Bonus Size

Last updated: July 2026 · Reviewed by the LaJugadaSegura00 editorial team · 5 min read

Slot bonuses are loud. RTP is quiet. But if you only ever chase the biggest welcome bonus and ignore RTP, you're optimizing for the wrong number.

What RTP actually means

Return to Player (RTP) is the percentage of all money wagered on a game that the game is mathematically designed to pay back over a very large number of spins. A slot with 96% RTP returns, on average, $96 for every $100 wagered across its full lifetime — not per session, not per player, but across the whole population of spins over years.

The remaining 4% is the house edge: the game's built-in margin. Every slot has one. There's no such thing as a slot with no house edge.

RTP is not a session guarantee

This is the part players misunderstand most: a 96% RTP slot can absolutely eat your entire balance in twenty minutes. RTP describes the theoretical long-run average, not what happens to you today. Short sessions are dominated by variance, not by RTP.

Why RTP matters more than a flashy bonus

A 200% welcome bonus on a 92% RTP slot can leave you worse off long-term than a smaller bonus on a 97% RTP game, once wagering requirements force you to spin through the bonus many times. The RTP compounds across every one of those required spins — the bonus size is a one-time number, RTP is what happens every single time you play afterward.

Where to actually find RTP

Licensed slots are required to disclose RTP, usually in the game's information or "i" menu, sometimes buried a few taps deep. If a site or game won't tell you the RTP at all, that's a bigger red flag than any missing bonus.

A simple rule of thumb

Look for games above 96% RTP when you have a choice, treat anything below 94% as a game you're playing for entertainment value rather than any expectation of return, and never let bonus size alone decide where you play.

Playing safe: A high RTP describes long-run behavior across millions of spins, not a guarantee for your session. Budget for variance either way.

Frequently asked questions

Does a high RTP mean I will win?

No. RTP is a long-run average across millions of spins, not a guarantee for your session. A 96% RTP slot can still lose your balance quickly due to variance.

What is a good RTP for a slot?

Look for 96% or higher when you have a choice. Below 94% you are mainly playing for entertainment rather than expecting any return.
